## The brief

A professor asked me for one place to document his work, his achievements and his history: his books and papers, his positions, his qualifications and the services he offers. It was my first bigger solo project, and my first for someone else.

## What I built

- **The pages.** Home, services, qualifications, positions and publications, with a shared bar along the top on every page.
- **Publications.** Books, journals and blogs, with the books grouped by period so you can browse decades of work.
- **The blog.** More than 15 posts, each a card that opens the full article, laid out with templates I designed myself.
- **A contact form.** My first web form, with Google reCAPTCHA to keep spam out and the messages routed to his email.

It's built in plain HTML and CSS and hosted free on GitHub Pages.

## What I'd do differently

The biggest lesson was about talking to the client. I didn't agree the pages and the content clearly at the start, so the build dragged on far longer than it needed to.

The second was reuse. Because it's almost all hand-written HTML, the shared parts, like the bar along the top and every blog card, are copied out again and again: hundreds of near-identical lines that all have to change together. A framework or even simple templates would have made that a fraction of the work. Not all of it went perfectly, but I got it running, and I was really proud of it.

## What I'd do next

- Rebuild it in Astro, so the navigation, the cards and the blog list are each written once.
- Move the posts into a content collection instead of hand-made cards.
